Li MengPostdoctoral researcher, University of Oslo

Learning state representations for biological dynamical systems.

Brains and embryos change over time, and we only ever see them through noisy, partial measurements. I develop self-supervised and reinforcement-learning methods that infer their hidden state and how it evolves, and apply them to EEG and brain-computer interfaces, early-embryo development, and genomics.

See publications Get in touch

Currently in the GENESIS convergence environment at the University of Oslo, with a staff page at the Institute of Basic Medical Sciences.

How can a model infer the hidden state of a living system, and how that state changes, from measurements that are noisy, incomplete, and mostly unlabeled?

The question behind each of the three directions below.

Research overview
2025 to 2026

Brain-state representations from EEG for dementia-risk screening

AI-Mind, Horizon 2020, at OsloMet, then eBRAIN-Health, Horizon Europe, at the University of Oslo

I was responsible for the machine learning in AI-Mind, on high-density EEG from a cohort of 1,000 participants in four countries. The self-supervised pre-training I led raised balanced accuracy by more than 10% in some experimental designs when labels were scarce, and time-aware models followed brain-network dynamics over four longitudinal visits. I presented the results to the European Commission reviewers in Rome, and later built the automated EEG pipeline for the same cohort.

AI-Mind projectRome assembly
2026

Speech decoding for an implantable brain-computer interface

Neuralicorn (西湖灵犀), algorithm lead

I led the company's speech-decoding experiment from start to finish: the experimental paradigm, brain-to-text decoding from neural-network phoneme decoding to language-model decoding, the end-to-end pipeline, and clinical data collection with the partner hospital.

2026 to now

Gene perturbation and cell states in the early embryo

GENESIS convergence environment, University of Oslo

I am responsible for designing the machine-learning models for gene perturbation, which model how perturbing a gene shifts cell and embryo states. Alongside them, I develop deep-learning methods that learn these states from single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, epigenetic markers, and live microscopy, to find what drives the self-organisation of the early mouse embryo.

GENESIS project
2026

Predicting gene knockdowns in cell lines never seen in training

Virtual Cell Challenge 2026, Arc Institute, team UiO

Given only control cells from three new cell lines, the task was to predict how each responds to 300 CRISPRi knockdowns. A knockdown's effect measured in other cell lines predicted a new line better than State, Arc's own perturbation model. So we built on measured effects from five public screens, and added single-cell foundation models and TabPFN on top, each judged against a matched control. Our entry finished 41st of 807 on the final leaderboard.
